# Profile: languages (English and other languages)

## What the book is for

Help a self-learner reach real use of the language, starting from what matters most. Base the syllabus on evidence, and say in the assessment what is supported and what is a myth.

## Syllabus principles (check them in the research)

- **Balance of the four strands** (Paul Nation): meaning-focused input (reading, listening), meaning-focused output (speaking, writing), language-focused learning (grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation) and fluency practice.
- **Lots of comprehensible input:** graded reading and listening every chapter; point to free sources and check that they exist.
- **High-frequency vocabulary first**; words in context, not lists alone.
- **Retrieval and spacing** for vocabulary and structures; every chapter reuses earlier words.
- **Output with feedback:** speaking and writing tasks with a checklist the reader can use alone, plus the AI tutor prompts as a conversation partner.
- Levels follow the CEFR (A1 → B1/B2) with "can do" goals ("can introduce yourself and ask simple questions").

## Practice

Each chapter: a short text or dialogue (written for the book), vocabulary in context, one structure, pronunciation, and tasks in four skills, with answers hidden and commented.

## Pronunciation

The IPA plus an approximation for speakers of the reader's native language (e.g. for Portuguese speakers: *works* /wɜːks/ ≈ "uérks"). The book has no audio: say where to listen (dictionaries with audio, the AI tutor's voice mode) without inventing links.

## Cautions

- Contrast with the reader's native language: false friends and typical mistakes of that language's speakers. This is why translated language books are adapted (`modes/TRANSLATE.md`).
- Vocabulary and example sentences as copyable text.
- Never reproduce song lyrics or long copyrighted texts; write the texts for the book.
- Myths to avoid: learning styles; "adults cannot learn a language".
