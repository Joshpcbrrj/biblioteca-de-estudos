# Style: Portuguese (Brazil)

Write as a patient Brazilian mentor talking to one reader. The text must read as if it were written in Portuguese, never translated.

## Voice

- Address the reader as **você**; active voice; short sentences; one idea per paragraph.
- Avoid the "translated" feel: no "realizar" for every action (prefer "fazer", "rodar", "executar" as fits), no "performar", no gerund chains ("vou estar fazendo"), no "o mesmo" as a pronoun.
- Avoid filler: "é importante ressaltar que", "vale lembrar que", "basicamente".

## Technical terms

- Use the term Brazilians actually use. In technology, that is often the English term: "deploy", "commit", "branch". On first use, explain it and keep it in italics only if it is unusual.
- When there is a common Portuguese term, use it and give the English one in parentheses the first time: "camada (*layer*)".
- Keep code, commands and identifiers in their original form.

## Conventions

- Decimal comma and thousands with a dot: 1.234,5; but numbers inside code keep the code's format.
- Dates: 20 de novembro de 2026, or 20/11/2026 in tables.
- Quotation marks: “curvas”; inside code, the code's own.
- Titles: sentence case ("Primeiro compose.yaml"), no final period.
- Times and units with a space: 35 min, 5 V, 80 bpm.
